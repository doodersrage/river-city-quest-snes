# River City Quest SNES: Roadmap

A full port of [River City Quest](https://github.com/doodersrage/river-city-quest) to a real
Super Nintendo ROM, built in stages. Each stage ends with a ROM that boots and plays.

## How the port works

- **Code:** C with [PVSnesLib](https://github.com/alekmaul/pvsneslib) (65816), with assembly where speed matters.
  GitHub Actions builds the ROM on every push and publishes it as the `latest` release.
- **Art:** `tools/export` runs the web game's own drawing code headlessly and exports every map and sprite as PNG.
  `tools/snesconv.py` turns those into SNES 4bpp tiles, palettes, tilemaps and collision.
  `tools/preview.py` decodes the SNES data back to PNG, so every conversion can be checked by eye.
- **Source of truth:** the web game. Maps, text and rules are extracted from it, not retyped.

## Hardware budget

| Resource | Limit | Plan |
|---|---|---|
| BG tiles per layer | 1,024 (4bpp) | Speckle limited to 4 variants per tile type. Busiest map: 466 tiles. |
| BG palettes | 8 × 15 colours | Greedy packing per map, global median cut if needed. Palette 0 keeps 3 colours for the text layer (BG3 shares CGRAM 1-3). |
| VRAM | 64 KB | BG1 tiles 0x0000, BG1 map 0x4000 (64×64), BG3 font 0x5000, BG3 map 0x5800, sprites 0x6000 (hero tiles 0-31, NPCs 32-127) |
| Work RAM | 128 KB | Game state, NPCs, enemies; save data to SRAM. WRAM is not cleared at power-on, so every variable is set in code; initialised globals land in bank $7F |
| ROM | 2 MB now, up to 4 MB | Each outdoor map is about 26 KB, each room about 15 KB (one 32 KB bank each) |
| Sprites | 128 on screen, 32 per scanline | 8x8 small (HUD, pickups) and 16x16 large; the hero and townspeople are 16x32 (two sprites). OBJ tiles: hero 0-3 and 16-19 (the frame on show), townspeople 32-63 (likewise), 64-127 free, enemies 128-159, paddle 160-223, HUD 224-255. Palettes: hero 0, NPCs 1-4, enemies 5-6, paddle and HUD 7 |
| Sound | SPC700, 64 KB audio RAM | Chiptunes rebuilt as tracker modules (snesmod) |

## Stages

1. **Walk Richmond** (done in first build). All 24 outdoor neighbourhoods with the web game's art, the hero with walk frames, collision, scrolling and fades between neighbourhoods.
2. **Text and talking** (done). BG3 font (Press Start 2P) and dialogue box, markers, landmarks, murals and lamps, 70 NPCs standing in place with their lines.
   Still to come with later stages: the journal menu for the 66 stories, Spanish text, NPCs walking around, and talk that changes with progress (gifts, trades and challenges currently show their opening lines only).
3. **Combat** (done). Paddle swing (the game's own paddle drawn at 16 angles) and hold-to-charge spin, hearts and coin HUD, the 7 outdoor enemy types with the web game's behaviours, hit stun and knockback, coin and heart drops, waking in Capitol Square.
   Not yet: cutting bushes, hit sparks and puffs, sound, the hero's hurt flash colour, and night enemies (stage 8).
4. **Doors and interiors** (done). Doors into all 24 single-room interiors and caves and back out, rooms centred on screen, bush cutting (from stage 3), the 26 chests with coins and heart charms, Carytown Goods and the 17th Street Market. 2 MB ROM.
   Not yet: the dog and furniture in the row house, cooking, the arcade, and what most shop items do (camera, rod, cards, satchel); they're recorded as bought for the stages that add them.
5. **Dungeon engine and Tredegar** (done). Room-to-room travel inside an area, rooms that stay cleared, chests that appear when a room is solved, small keys per dungeon, locked doors, gates tied to solved rooms, pits and iron posts, the Grappling Hook, projectiles, a 48x48 boss built from 9 sprites in the second OBJ name table, boss stairs and heart containers, waking at the dungeon entrance. Tredegar Iron Works and the Iron Foreman are complete.
6. **Items and the other dungeons, together (done).** Each dungeon needs the item it hides, so they come in pairs: the Quarry and Blasting Powder (done: cracked walls, rubble, Old Snapper, the Granite Keystone, SELECT to switch items, keg drops), the Pyramid and the Gas Lantern (done: the Archivist's gift, braziers and lamp-lit rooms, the Cast-Iron Shield, reflected shots, the Wraith, the Iron Keystone; dark rooms come with lighting in stage 8), the Poe cellar (done: the Gold-Bug, pickups lying in rooms, the Raven, the Brick Keystone; boss sprite cells are now packed so four bosses fit the second OBJ table), the Carillon (done: the trading quest and kayak, river crossing, the Carillon Key, push blocks and switches, the Bronze Bell, the Bronze Keystone, heart pieces), the Conservatory (done: the Watering Can, sprouts and vine bridges, the Titan Arum; a grown sprout is walkable here, since in the web game the stalk blocks the only way onto its vine), the item menu and gear (done: START menu, Baseballs, Running Shoes and the race bib, the Bicycle at about 2 px a frame everywhere rather than faster on paths, Ginger Beer and refills, shop restocks and the Big Satchel; the Fishing Rod and Camera come with the minigames and town life), and the Church Hill Tunnel finale (done: the keystone door, the Flooded Siding, the Signal Room, the Richmond Vampire, and the ending: the lamps come back on in five neighbourhoods, then the credits. The relight is shown by daylight for now; lamps lit at night come with lighting in stage 8. There's no name entry yet, so the credits star Rory).
7. **Music and sound (done).** Every web game track rebuilt for the SPC700, plus sound effects. tools/mkaudio.py reads the web game's own songs (lead, bass and drum lines) and writes one Impulse Tracker module per song, with looped single-cycle square and triangle samples and one-shot drums, one row per eighth note; SNESMOD (PVSnesLib's driver) plays them from a soundbank in ROM banks 52 and up. Sixteen sound effects (swing, hit, clank, hurt, defeat, coin, heart, talk, menu, fuse, explosion, item jingle, secret jingle, boss shot, slam, fanfare) are the web game's sfx() and tune() calls rendered to samples. Each place plays the song the web game picks: the town theme outdoors and in lit buildings, its own theme south of the river, the forge in the Tredegar works, the dungeon theme in dungeons and caves, the boss theme during a fight, and the victory theme over the ending. The title and night themes are in the soundbank for stages 8 and 11. Changing song uploads a module to the sound chip, which takes a moment; it happens during the screen fade between places.
8. **Day, night and weather (done).** A day is ten minutes, as in the web game, starting at 9am; dusk comes on from 5pm, full dark from 8pm to 5am, then dawn. Darkness is colour math (subtracting a fixed colour, halved at full dark) from BG1, the backdrop and sprite palettes 4-7, except inside the colour window: two windows, each a circle of light whose edges come from an HDMA table (channels 4 and 5) built per scanline by a small 65816 routine (src/lightcircle.asm). One circle follows you (bigger with the Gas Lantern), the other sits on the nearest lit brazier, wall torch, street lamp or lit window. Rooms the web game leaves unlit (dungeons, caves, museums) are always dark, the Church Hill Tunnel in a purple dark. Weather changes every 50-120 seconds outdoors: rain (falling sprites, a darker sky), fog (washed out) or snow (drifting flakes, washed out). The web game weighs the weather by the real season; the SNES has no calendar, so it uses the year's average. The night theme plays outdoors after dark, and the ending's lamps come back on at night. The ROM is now FastROM (code runs at 3.58 MHz), which pays for all of this: the game holds 60 fps everywhere except walking through rain or snow at night, which holds about 90%. Still to do: the lit-window sprites the web game paints on buildings at night, the frogs that come out in the rain (a stage 9 sidequest), and rain and wind sounds.
9. **Town life (done).** The web game's townspeople ("Locals") wander within 40 px of where they stand, a step at a time between pauses, never into you or a car; the named people stay put. Cars (red, yellow or blue) and the Pulse bus run in the lanes the web game gives eight neighbourhoods, wrap around at the edges, and stop with a short horn while you stand in the lane ahead of them; they block you like a wall. Pigeons sit where the web game puts them and fly off when you come within 30 px, then come back once you've wandered away. Their art loads into the boss area of the second OBJ table on outdoor maps, which never have a boss (boss cells shared between frames now, so the bosses still fit). The trading quest was done in stage 6; the bateau quest is here: three part pickups (oar and pole at Pony Pasture, the tiller on the island off Ancarrow's Landing) and the river historian's reward (50 coins, a heart charm, the Golden Lure for stage 10's fishing). To keep the frame rate, town life runs on alternate frames (traffic on one, people and pigeons on the other, each moving twice as far) and only rewrites a sprite when it changes; by day the game still holds 60 fps (it dips a little while you lean on a stopped car), and walking through rain at night runs at about 75-90%, close to where stage 8 left it. Two new sound effects (the horn, a flap). The sound bank passed 32 KB and is split over banks 52 and 53. Still to do: the frogs that come out on rainy nights (with the naturalist's frog count, in stage 10).
10. **Challenges and minigames (done).** In parts:
   - **The neighbourhood challenges (done).** All 14, from the web game's own table: collect (Shag Flies, Lost Taps, Melon Roundup, Lost Lures with the Gold-Bug, River Cleanup), race to a flag (Catch the Bus, the Monument Avenue 10K, Last Trolley), defeat (Market Rats, Restless Spirits, Crab Catch: up to six at a time, more as you beat them), Light the Gas Lamps (the lantern on B at each of Church Hill's lamps) and the Mural Hunt (A at each mural). The challenger offers it with a question in the text box (LET'S GO! / NOT NOW); a countdown and count sit at the top of the screen; walking off the map calls it off. A win is a Richmond city pin and 25 coins (10 for a repeat); all 14 pins make the paddle hit twice as hard. A "!" blinks over anyone whose pin you haven't won. The Tour Guide's history quiz asks three questions about stories in your journal, which fills as you read markers. Tokens use the challenger's sprite palette (palette 7 has no greens or blues). Also in this part: button taps that land in a long frame now always count (a VBlank routine collects them; PVSnesLib only reads the pad at the end of a frame that finished in time), which fixed occasional lost presses in busy neighbourhoods. Not yet: the paddle doesn't change colour when it turns gold, Church Hill's lamps don't stay lit at night after the Lamplighter's challenge, and only markers (not people) add journal stories.
   - **Minigames (done).** Each takes over the screen: the web game's court or field rendered once by the exporter onto BG1 (256x200, 12 px down, so the score line and messages sit on BG3 above and below it), 8x8 sprites for the ball, the bat or racket (a line of dots at the swing angle, from the sine table) and the free-throw needle, and the result as a text box back in the neighbourhood, with the coins and a best score. Done: **batting practice** (the Ballplayer, once his pin is won: ten pitches, swing timing decides home run, hit, foul or miss), **Rams free throws** (the needle speeds up each shot; 2 coins a basket) and **Ashe Center tennis** (the ball machine speeds up as you return; three misses ends it), all with the web game's numbers. Also done: **the soapbox derby, the Capital Trail time trial and the Raceway dash**, one scrolling-road game with three courses. Each course is a 256x512 strip on BG1 (the whole 64x64 map, so vertical scrolling loops it; the web game's 24 px road dashes became 32 so the pattern divides 512), with the player and obstacles as 16x16 sprite cells in their own palettes. Speed, steering, bumps, puddles and oil, moving cars, course lengths and medal times are the web game's; a best time is kept. And **Stone House pinball** (done): the web game's table, bumpers, guides and flippers in fixed point (1/64 px), its gravity, wall damping, bumper kick, speed cap and stuck-ball nudge; the ball moves in quarter steps rather than the web game's halves so a fast ball can't slip through a 3 px guide. The flippers are rows of dots at nine angles; a lit bumper is a sprite over the dark one. That finishes the minigames. Found on the way: work done inside the VBlank hook (rcqVBlank) mustn't index arrays, since the data bank there isn't the one the C code expects, so the minigame's score line is laid out in the main loop and only copied in VBlank. Also fixed here: new variables are cleared at power-on (SNES work RAM isn't zeroed, and a stray minigame flag switched night off).
   - **Fishing, landmark cards and the frog count (done).** The Fishing Rod (Carytown Goods, 30 coins) casts with B at open water (lakes and ponds are now their own collision cell, so the ROM can tell them from walls; the James counts too). The web game's three phases: wait (the Golden Lure shortens it), a 28-frame bite, then a reel where every A pulls the fish in but tightens the line, and the fish makes runs; its distance and tension are bars in the text box. Fish by the web game's weights: bluegill, bass, catfish (at night), shad (in the rain) and sturgeon (only in the James, three times likelier with the lure); coins as in the web game. Landmark cards: twelve, from the five bosses (in their last words), card packs (20 coins, never a repeat), the first sturgeon and the frog count; a Landmark Cards page at the end of the START menu lists them, and A reads one. Frogs: on rainy days, up to three at a time hop about the grass beside the water; catch five for the naturalist in Byrd Park for 40 coins and the Maymont card. Frogs use an enemy sprite palette, since the item palette has no green. Not yet: the fish tank and the other row-house furniture.
   - **The camera and the photo album (done).** The Camera (Carytown Goods, 25 coins) on B photographs the nearest of the 30 captioned landmarks within 150 px, as the web game does. The album (the last line of the START menu once you have the camera) is one screen: the exporter draws every landmark's thumbnail as the web game does (thumbOf) into a 5 x 6 grid, and the ROM writes a blank block with a question mark over the ones you haven't taken. A cursor moves over them, with the caption below. Not yet: the web game's night and weather tints on a photo, and the big view and sharing.
11. **Saves and polish (done).** Battery-backed SRAM (the header asks for 8 KB with a battery) and three save slots of 1 KB each: a magic word, everything that is progress in one fixed order (src/save.c walks the same list to save, load and measure), and a checksum, so a slot that doesn't check out reads as empty. Arriving in any map saves, as the web game autosaves; the save happens during the fade, while the screen is black. The title screen is the web game's skyline and logo, rendered by the exporter (the moon moved up into the corner so the logo's Y stays readable), with the slots and choices on BG3; its rows are copied straight after VBlank starts, before the sound driver's turn. Name entry is a grid of letters with DEL and OK (Rory by default, up to 8 letters, stored as Ada rather than ADA). The opening is the web game's four intro panels as one text box, with the name put in where the converter left a marker byte, and the credits read "Starring" and your name. The play test starts a new game from the title, names the hero, and at the end switches the console off and on (a fresh emulator keeping only SRAM) to check CONTINUE.
   Not yet: a test on real hardware with a flash cart. The archivist's last line in the ending still calls the story "Rory and the Fog" whatever your name is.

12. **A later-SNES look (in progress, branch stage12-art).** Toward Chrono Trigger and Secret of Mana: shaded colour ramps, taller characters, depth and effects, a richer HUD. The art is the SNES edition's own; the web game keeps its look. The ROM is now 4 MB, and each map's tiles get a bank of their own (shaded tiles roughly double a map's tile count, still under the 1,024 a layer allows).
   - **12a Tiles (mostly done).** tools/export/snes_style.js replaces the web game's drawing functions during export: grass, flowers, paving, cobbles, sand, the grass edge, trees, slate roofs, brick, interior walls, the James and the ponds (deep ramps, wave crests, shadow under the banks, foam at the shore, lily pads on ponds), plank boardwalks and bridges, and boulders. Colours come from fixed ramps (shadows lean violet, highlights warm). Buildings three cells tall show an upper storey with windows under an eave, and buildings cast shadows down and to the right. Palettes: shaded art has too many colour combinations to pack exactly into eight palettes, so maps that don't pack are clustered (tools/snesconv.py cluster_palettes: tiles assigned to the palette that reproduces them best, each palette's 15 colours fitted to its tiles and snapped to colours the art really uses, repeated until it settles). The web game's hand-drawn landmarks (the Capitol, Main Street Station, the Broad Street stores and the rest) are shaded automatically: each is drawn alone on a clear layer, every shape gets a lit top-left edge and a shadowed lower-right one, its foot darkens where it meets the ground, and its silhouette is thrown as a shadow down and right. Inside, floors are long boards or bevelled flagstones with the walls' shadow across them. Props (lamps, benches, signs, fences, gravestones, statues, furniture) get the same treatment per cell: drawn whole and as just their ground on a scratch layer to separate the prop, shaded, given a short cast shadow (the web game's translucent shadows are left out), and put back.
   - **12b Sprites (hero, townspeople and enemies done).** The hero is 16x32 (tools/art/hero32.py): two sprites, three frames a direction, and only the frame on show is in VRAM. heroVBlank copies a new one at the start of VBlank when it changes, as later SNES games do; that frees the sprite memory for bigger characters. Townspeople are built from the same templates with their own colours from the web game (hat, shirt, and skin, hair and trousers by the web game's hash; about half go bareheaded) and stream the same way: each of the four on a map has a 16x32 slot (OBJ tiles 32+4k and 48+4k, so 64-127 are free now), one person's new frame is copied a VBlank, and wanderers walk with a step cycle. Sprite slots: the hero's head is OAM 13 (above his body), people's heads 36-39, the coin digits moved to 60-62. The seven outdoor enemies are redrawn (tools/art/enemies.py) with two frames each at OBJ tiles 64-127, in a warm and a cool palette (the frog borrows the cool one, which carries its greens); the minigames borrow those tiles, so they reload with every map. Next: bosses, and swing poses.
   - **12c Depth and effects (animation done).** Animated tiles: the exporter renders every map at the web game's four animation frames, and the converter gives each tile that changes its own slot at the end of the map's tile set (one per different four-frame tile, palettes chosen to cover all four frames) with the frames in a section of their own. rcqAnim starts a map's animation; every 10 frames the next frame is copied over those tiles, 32 tiles a VBlank (animTick queues, animVBlank copies). 75 maps animate, up to 121 tiles each (water, rapids, flames, banners). The minigames, album and title switch it off while they hold the background. Walk-behind treetops and rooftops on a second layer were left out: every tree and building is a single cell, so the 16x32 characters already overlap them the right way. Still to do: see-through fog and ghosts with colour math, and shadows under characters.
   - **12d HUD and text box (done).** Every window (dialogue, menus, the shop, questions, cards, the title's slots) has a bevelled white frame, a shadowed font (the shadow falls a pixel down and right, which also opens a dark row between lines) and a fill that fades from Chrono Trigger blue to deep navy: HDMA channels 2 and 3 rewrite the two text fills (CGRAM 1 and 5) every other line while a window is open (gradBuild, textVBlank; light.c shares HDMAEN through hdmaLight and hdmaText). The text layer owns BG palette 0's first seven colours: fill, shadow and white for text palette 0, a spare grey for the map, then fill, shadow and gold for text palette 1, so gold text is the same 96 glyph tiles with the palette bit (FONT_GOLD is 1 + 0x400) and 96 tiles were freed. The HUD's hearts and coin are redrawn (tools/art/hud.py).

13. **Double resolution (in progress, branch stage13, from stage12-art).** The maps were laid out for the web game's 16 px people, so the 16x32 characters of stage 12 looked like giants next to 12 px doorways. Every cell becomes 32 px: a doorway is 26 px, a two-storey house about two people tall. The SNES edition's maps diverge from the web game from here.
   Measured before starting (the current art doubled with Scale2x): a whole map needs 1,000-1,400 different 8x8 tiles, over the 1,024 a background can address, but any one screen needs at most about 410. So the engine streams tiles, as later SNES games do.
   - **13a Engine.** Cells of 32 px: every world coordinate doubles (positions, doors, exits, people, enemies, pickups, chests, cars, birds), collision stays one byte a cell. The map streams as you scroll: a ring-buffer tilemap in VRAM, and a cache of up to 1,024 tiles holding what's on and near the screen (a count of uses per slot; a tile scrolling into view that isn't cached takes a free slot and is copied in VBlank). Each map keeps all its tiles and its full tilemap in ROM. The converter outputs every map at double size, at first the current art through Scale2x. The play test and sneslab move to the new scale. *Done:* game logic keeps its 16 px cells and the screen draws them at 32 px (a 128x112 map-pixel view). Maps are metatiles (4x4 tiles each) with up to 2,048 tiles in ROM; the loaded area is 35x31 tiles, one tile of margin all round, and new edges load 16 tiles a frame ahead of the screen (src/stream.c, with the per-tile work in src/streamvb.asm on the direct page). A shadow of the ring's slots makes dropping a tile a lookup-free decrement. Walking speed matches stage 12 (about 42 of 60 frames sideways in the Fan, 38 up and down; both builds share the same bottleneck, in the per-frame game logic, for later). All 227 play-test checks pass.
   - **13b Native art.** Ground, paving, water, roofs, brick, trees and interiors redrawn at 32 px; landmarks and props through Scale2x and then shaded. *First pass done (branch stage13b):* the exporter paints each map twice at 16 px, whole and as bare ground, and sorts every pixel into ground, shadow or object (tools/export/snes_hires.js). At 32 px, grass, flowers, cobbles, pavement, sand, lawn edges, floors, trees, slate roofs, brick fronts, dungeon walls and torches, water and boardwalks are drawn natively; other ground goes through Scale2x; shadows are reapplied as the shade of whatever is under them; and objects (windows, doors, landmarks, props) go on top through Scale2x with transparent edges. Props stand on whatever ground their neighbours do. Water animates only a sun glint, and a map with more than 96 moving tiles stays still. The busiest map needs 1,468 of its 2,048 tiles. Still Scale2x: clapboard and styled fronts, the ironworks' brick walls, rapids, boulders and the landmarks themselves.
   - **13c Sprites and combat.** The 16x32 characters keep their size (right for 32 px cells); small animals stay 16x16; bosses grow; the paddle's reach, walking speed and enemy ranges are retuned. *Partly done (branch stage13c):* cars and the Pulse bus are drawn at 32 px tall through Scale2x, four 16x16 sprites a piece. They take OAM 40-59, the boss's and its shots' slots, which no street map uses. The boss area of VRAM holds one of two sheets per map: two car colours, or one colour and the bus. The paddle is 32 px too and swings out to where its hits land. Its art streams by angle (tiles 160-163, 176-179, 192-195, 208-211; OAM 15, 11, 12, 63), like the hero's frames. Still to do: bosses (48x48 now, 1.5 cells; 96x96 would need 36 sprites or a bigger sprite size), the bomb's blast, and any retuning of walking speed and enemy ranges after a play on hardware.
   - **13e Speed (branch stage13e).** Profiling (patching one function at a time to return at once, and counting game loops per 60 frames) found the frame's biggest cost was collision: every enemy step tests four corners, and tcc turned each test into a multiply, signed shifts and a chain of compares. solidAt, riverAt and enBlocked are now assembly (src/collide.asm), and the hero's test skips the push blocks where there are none. Walking in the Fan went from 42-44 game loops a second to 56-60, and the busiest test spot from 170 to 201 per 240 frames. What's left is mostly updEnemy (its divisions) and the enemies' movement.
     Bosses are 96x96 now, drawn on BG2 the way later SNES games draw big bosses. At that size they need more sprites and sprite memory than are spare. Their 48x48 frames go through Scale2x, and each boss's distinct 8x8 tiles (27 to 114) take slots from the map's tile cache when the room loads. BG palette 7 is the boss's (boss rooms pack their own art into seven), and the 12x12 tilemap sits at VRAM 0x5C00. BG2's scroll puts the boss where it stands, with tile priority so it's above the floor and under the sprites. A new frame, a turn or the screen's edge rewrites the tilemap in VBlank; columns and rows that would wrap round onto the far side are left out. Moving the boss only changes the scroll. The boss stays bright in dark rooms, as the sprite boss did (BG2 isn't in the colour math).
     The powder keg's blast is 64 px, Scale2x of its two 32 px frames. Each frame is 16 cells (in the old blast's cells and the spare ones in the paddle area), streamed in over two VBlanks when it shows: 2 KB in one ran past the end of VBlank and lost its last cells. It uses 16 spare sprite slots. With that, everything in the stage 13 plan is at the new scale.
   - **13d Puzzles and effects.** Push blocks, switches, braziers, the light circles, weather and the boss rooms at the new scale. *Mostly done (branch stage13d):* the light circles are twice the web's radii (hero 64 at night, 108 with the lantern; 76 and 120 in dark rooms; braziers 88, torches 92, lamps 96, windows 30), from new half-width tables. Push blocks are 32 px: Scale2x of the item cell, made by the converter and loaded into OBJ tiles 164-215 (around the streamed paddle), four sprites a block in OAM 100-115. Those are weather's slots, so blocks only touch them in rooms that have blocks. Switches, braziers and doors are map tiles, so they were already at 32 px. Left: bosses and the bomb blast (see 13c).

## Known gaps to revisit

- **Palette quality:** stage 12's palette clustering replaced the old greedy packing and global colour cut for busy maps; a little colour is still shared between neighbouring materials on the busiest maps.
- **Variety:** 4 speckle variants per tile type loses a little variety (fewer red-berry trees). 6 to 8 variants may still fit.
- **Animated tiles:** water and flame frames from the web game are not exported yet (frames 1 to 3).

## Performance notes

- Every sprite goes through `sprPut` / `sprHide` (main.c), which skip OAM calls for sprites that stay hidden. Calling `oamSetEx` and `oamSet` for every slot every frame cost about 20% of the frame budget.
- The HUD redraws only when health, coins or the B item change.
- Stage 12: sprPut remembers what each slot was last set to and skips oamSet when nothing changed. The 16x32 characters go through sprPair (src/sprpair.asm), which writes both OAM entries in one call: two C calls a character cost about 7% of the frame while the camera scrolls. Walking in the Fan with three townspeople: 216 of 240 frames (stage 11: 220). Most of what's left is the wanderers' own update in life.c (about 20 frames in 240).
- The play test measures nothing yet, but `frameCount` against emulator frames is the check to use: 120/120 outdoors and in boss rooms as of the Quarry.
